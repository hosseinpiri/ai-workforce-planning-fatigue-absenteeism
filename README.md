# AI-Driven Workforce Planning and Scheduling under Fatigue and Absenteeism

Saeed Aramesh, Babak Abbasi, Ron Wakefield (RMIT University) and Hossein Piri (Haskayne School of Business, University of Calgary).

Working paper, prepared for submission to *Production and Operations Management*.

- [Download the PDF](AI_Driven_Workforce_Planning_Fatigue_Absenteeism.pdf)

## Abstract

Residential construction projects are delivered through labor-intensive tasks under tight deadlines, yet current project scheduling and staff planning models usually treat workforce capacity as fixed and overlook human-centric factors such as labor fatigue, fairness in fatigue exposure, fatigue-induced quality degradation, and fatigue-related absenteeism. This paper introduces an integrated personnel staffing and project scheduling model for residential construction that progresses to a human-centric-aware planning framework. A fatigue activation mechanism reveals the operational impact of assigned workload on workforce fatigue, and the human-centric factors are incorporated sequentially to isolate how each reshapes staffing and scheduling decisions. Fairness restricts the feasible region, quality degradation introduces endogenous rework costs, and absenteeism risk links workload allocation to effective working capacity through a fatigue-capacity feedback mechanism. Incorporating sick-leave risk can improve work quality relative to a quality-only framework, because the model anticipates future capacity loss and reallocates workload more conservatively.

**Keywords:** Fatigue; Absenteeism; Staffing; Scheduling; Fairness; Construction
